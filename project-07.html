The Challenge

The business was profitable on an annual basis, but monthly cash availability remained uneven.

Management therefore needed more than a traditional profit and loss view. The key question was whether operating profitability was converting into sufficient and predictable cash to support the business.

The project connected P&L performance with cash-flow behaviour, working capital, break-even requirements and management scenarios.

Scope of Work

The analysis combined financial performance and liquidity indicators into a single decision framework.

Financial Performance

Annual revenue reached 1,080,000 €, producing gross profit of 432,000 € and a gross margin of 40%.

Operating expenses totalled 360,000 €, leaving EBITDA of 72,000 € and an operating margin of 6.7%.

The company was therefore profitable, but the operating margin provided limited protection against delayed collections, unexpected expenditure or periods of weaker sales.

Cash-Flow Intelligence

The cash-flow view identified a significant liquidity trough during the middle of the year.

Closing cash declined from 74,000 € in January to a low point of 42,000 € in June before recovering to 91,000 € by year-end.

The pattern demonstrated an important management distinction: a profitable company can still experience operating pressure when cash enters the business materially later than costs leave it.

Working Capital

Average customer collection timing was modelled at 52 days, while payroll and a significant share of operating costs were paid within the same month.

This created a structural cash-conversion gap.

Reducing average collection time from 52 to approximately 42 days was estimated to release around 30,000 € of cash from working capital, subject to sales mix and invoice timing.

Break-even Analysis

At the existing cost and margin structure, monthly operating break-even was approximately 82,000 € of revenue.

The break-even point was treated as a management floor rather than a commercial target.

A safer operating threshold of approximately 90,000 € per month was recommended to provide additional protection against collection delays and normal cost volatility.

Scenario Analysis

Three operating scenarios were developed to test the resilience of profitability and liquidity.

Downside Scenario 990,000 € revenue, 36,000 € EBITDA and 48,000 € projected closing cash.
Base Scenario 1,080,000 € revenue, 72,000 € EBITDA and 91,000 € projected closing cash.
Improvement Scenario 1,125,000 € revenue, 108,000 € EBITDA and 132,000 € projected closing cash.

Management KPIs

Monthly financial reporting should connect profitability and liquidity instead of monitoring them independently.

The recommended management indicators include revenue, gross margin, EBITDA margin, monthly break-even coverage, closing cash, collection days, receivables over 60 days and a rolling 13-week cash forecast.

A minimum operating cash buffer of 50,000 € was also introduced as an early-warning management threshold.

90-Day Plan

Days 1-30 - Visibility: introduce a rolling 13-week cash forecast, receivables ageing review and weekly collection priorities.

Days 31-60 - Conversion: tighten invoice follow-up, address weak payment patterns and introduce deposits or milestone billing where commercially appropriate.

Days 61-90 - Margin: review low-margin work, pricing exceptions and fixed-cost commitments before approving additional recurring expenditure.

Final Recommendation

PROTECT LIQUIDITY BEFORE EXPANDING THE FIXED COST BASE

The company is profitable, but the cash cycle creates avoidable operating pressure.

Management should improve collection speed, maintain a minimum 50,000 € operating cash buffer and use the 82,000 € monthly break-even level as an early-warning threshold.

Additional fixed costs should be approved only when the rolling cash forecast remains above the minimum liquidity buffer under both the base and downside scenarios.
