New Market Entry & Commercial Expansion

The Challenge

A Greek B2B services business was modelled at the point of evaluating expansion into a second European market.

The challenge was to determine where expansion made the most commercial sense before committing significant capital, establishing a permanent local presence or increasing the fixed-cost base.

Cyprus, Romania and Bulgaria were assessed through a common decision framework covering demand, accessibility, competition, commercial fit and operating complexity.

Scope of Work

The project combined market intelligence with commercial and financial decision modelling to create an actionable entry plan.

Market Screening Demand, accessibility, competitive intensity and commercial fit.
Competitor Intelligence Offer structures, pricing signals and market positioning.
Customer Prioritization Target segment, buyer profile and initial value proposition.
Entry Model Direct sales, partnerships and hybrid market-entry scenarios.
Commercial Economics Launch budget, revenue ramp, conversion and break-even thresholds.

Market Selection

Cyprus achieved the highest overall attractiveness score at 84/100, compared with 73/100 for Romania and 67/100 for Bulgaria.

The market provided the strongest balance of commercial accessibility, business familiarity, manageable operating complexity and compatibility with a remote-first B2B service model.

Romania offered stronger theoretical demand potential, but the additional entry complexity reduced its overall attractiveness during the initial expansion stage.

Priority Segment

The recommended initial target was established SMEs with approximately 10 to 75 employees that already purchase external professional services but do not maintain the full capability internally.

This segment combines relatively short decision cycles with sufficient project value to support a remote delivery model.

The proposed value proposition focuses on measurable commercial improvement and execution support rather than broad consulting positioning.

Entry Model

A hybrid entry model was selected, combining direct commercial outreach with a limited network of referral and implementation partners.

Direct outreach provides control over positioning, customer learning and pricing, while local partners improve market access and credibility.

No permanent local office is recommended during the validation period. A physical presence should only be reconsidered once recurring monthly revenue and qualified pipeline demonstrate sufficient commercial traction.

Commercial Model

The Year 1 revenue target was set at 154,000 €, supported by a gradual commercial ramp rather than immediate scale.

Market-specific monthly revenue is expected to reach approximately 27,000 € by Month 12, while the initial validation-phase launch budget is capped at 24,000 €.

Commercial break-even is expected once monthly market-specific revenue consistently exceeds approximately 11,500 €, assuming the lean operating structure is maintained.

90-Day Roadmap

Days 1-30: finalize the target-account universe, localize messaging, approach initial prospects and identify potential referral partners.

Days 31-60: expand commercial outreach, issue initial proposals, test pricing and positioning, document objections and activate qualified partners.

Days 61-90: evaluate conversion, pipeline quality, project economics and delivery capacity before deciding whether to accelerate, adjust or pause expansion.

Decision Gates

Expansion beyond the validation phase requires market-specific monthly revenue of at least 11,500 €, proposal conversion of at least 30%, gross margin above 55% and a qualified pipeline capable of supporting the following 90 days.

The model also prevents premature fixed-cost expansion by linking further investment directly to demonstrated commercial traction.

Final Recommendation

GO - CYPRUS FIRST

Proceed with a six-month lean validation phase using a hybrid direct-sales and partner-led model.

No permanent office should be established at launch. Further capital deployment should depend on revenue, conversion, margin and pipeline thresholds rather than expansion targets alone.
