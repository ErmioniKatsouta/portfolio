Sales Intelligence & Executive Reporting

Sales intelligence framework designed to transform fragmented commercial data into clear KPIs, performance trends, customer insights and management-ready reporting.

The Challenge

Sales information was available across multiple commercial records, but management lacked a single structured view of performance.

Monthly revenue totals alone could not explain whether growth came from new customers, repeat business, stronger channels or temporary large transactions.

The objective was to move from descriptive reporting toward decision-oriented sales intelligence: what changed, why it changed, whether targets were being achieved and where commercial attention should be focused next.

Scope of Work

The project structured a management reporting framework connecting sales performance with customer, channel, target and pipeline data.

Data Structure Organization of sales records into a consistent reporting structure.
Sales KPIs Revenue, growth, target attainment, average deal and repeat sales.
Performance Analysis Monthly and quarterly sales trends against targets.
Customer Intelligence New versus repeat revenue and customer concentration.
Channel Analysis Direct, partner, inbound and existing-client contribution.
Executive Reporting Management KPIs, exceptions, insights and actions.

Sales Performance

Annual sales reached 901,000 €, with performance strengthening during the second half of the year.

Monthly sales increased from 61,000 € in January to 92,000 € in December, while the business finished above its annual commercial target.

The reporting framework separates sustainable growth from temporary deal effects so that management can evaluate the quality of commercial momentum rather than relying only on headline revenue.

Customer Intelligence

Repeat clients generated 54% of annual sales, creating a stable commercial base while new customers contributed the remaining 46%.

The top 10 customers represented 43% of total sales. The level remained manageable but was included as a recurring management KPI to prevent future dependence on a small number of accounts.

Average deal value was also incorporated into the reporting framework to support pricing, customer and channel decisions.

Channel Performance

Direct sales represented 38% of revenue and remained the largest commercial channel.

Partner referrals generated 27%, inbound activity 19% and expansion from existing clients 16%.

Partner-sourced opportunities were identified as a strategic growth area because they combined lower acquisition effort with attractive deal quality. The recommendation was therefore to track partner performance as a separate commercial channel.

Pipeline & Conversion

Proposal win rate was measured at 31%, with material differences between opportunity sources.

The analysis recommended increasing qualified pipeline coverage from approximately 2.4x to at least 3.0x of the next 90-day sales target.

This changes management focus from raw lead volume toward qualified opportunities with a realistic probability of conversion.

Executive Reporting

The proposed management report was deliberately kept concise. Its purpose is to answer five commercial questions every month.

Are sales on target? What changed versus the previous period? Which customers and channels explain the change? Is the forward pipeline sufficient? What action is required before the next management review?

The resulting reporting structure combines headline KPIs, performance trends, customer and channel contribution, pipeline exceptions and a short management action register.

Management Actions

Partner reporting: track partner-sourced pipeline, conversion and average deal value independently.

Pipeline coverage: maintain at least 3.0x qualified coverage against the next 90-day sales target.

Repeat revenue: flag important accounts showing declining order frequency or commercial value.

Conversion analysis: separate poor lead quality from pricing, proposal and sales-process issues.

Reporting discipline: use one KPI definition set and one management view across recurring commercial reviews.

Final Recommendation

KEEP THE REPORTING LAYER SIMPLE AND ACTION-ORIENTED

The value of sales intelligence is not the number of charts produced. It is the ability to identify performance changes early and connect them with a specific commercial decision.

Management should therefore prioritize consistent KPI definitions, stronger pipeline visibility and disciplined interpretation of customer and channel performance.
