Commercial Performance & Profitability Diagnostic

Management diagnostic connecting sales performance, customer economics, pricing and service-level profitability to identify where revenue creates value, where margin is lost and which commercial actions should be prioritized.

The Challenge

The project models an established B2B services company experiencing revenue growth without a proportional improvement in profitability and cash generation.

Management needed to understand which customers and service lines were genuinely creating economic value and where pricing, discounting and delivery effort were reducing margin.

The objective was not simply to report sales performance, but to identify the commercial decisions capable of improving profit quality without depending on aggressive revenue growth.

Scope of Work

The diagnostic connected revenue, customer, pricing and service data into a single management decision framework.

Service Profitability

Core Services represented the largest source of revenue, while Premium Services generated the strongest margin quality.

Low-margin Custom Work accounted for approximately 19.1% of revenue but only 10.5% of gross profit, demonstrating that revenue contribution alone overstated its commercial value.

The analysis therefore recommended protecting premium delivery capacity while repricing, standardizing or selectively declining custom work that falls below the minimum margin threshold.

Customer Economics

The top 10 customers generated 58% of annual revenue. The level of concentration remained manageable, but several high-volume accounts also received above-average commercial discounts.

Customers were separated into four management groups: Protect, Grow, Reprice and Review.

This structure allows management to align pricing, service effort, retention activity and commercial attention with the actual economic contribution of each account.

Pricing Leakage

Approximately 41,000 € of annual list-price value was being given away through discounts and commercial exceptions.

Around 18,500 € was classified as addressable leakage because the discounting was not clearly linked to volume, contract duration or strategic account value.

The recommended commercial control requires documented justification for discounts above 8% and management approval for engagements expected to fall below 30% gross margin.

Profit Improvement

The analysis identified an estimated 46,000 € annualized profit-improvement opportunity without requiring equivalent additional revenue.

Approximately 18,000 € could be generated through repricing low-margin custom work, 11,000 € through tighter discount control, 10,000 € through a stronger Premium Services mix and 7,000 € through improved scope and delivery control.

This shifts management attention from revenue volume toward revenue quality and contribution.

Management KPIs

The proposed management scorecard sets a gross-margin target above 40%, increases the Premium Services share of revenue above 30% and reduces low-margin custom work below 12% of total revenue.

Projects closing below 30% gross margin should fall below 5% of total activity, while unstructured annual discount leakage should be reduced to less than 7,500 €.

Customer concentration should remain below 60% of revenue for the top 10 accounts.

90-Day Plan

Days 1-30 - Control: introduce margin floors, discount approval rules and service-level profitability monitoring.

Days 31-60 - Reprice: redesign low-margin custom offers, update proposal structures and renegotiate selected recurring accounts.

Days 61-90 - Shift Mix: direct commercial effort toward Premium Services and profitable Core Services while reducing resources allocated to persistently low-contribution activity.

Final Recommendation

IMPROVE PROFIT QUALITY BEFORE CHASING ADDITIONAL REVENUE

The business does not have a primary sales-volume problem. The main issue is revenue quality, concentrated in low-margin custom work, discount exceptions and inconsistent commercial controls.

Management should target gross margin above 40% before materially increasing fixed sales or delivery capacity.
